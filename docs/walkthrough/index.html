Design Your Level

A 15-minute live activity at https://play.mottonote.app. Teams of 4 describe a game level that uses all four aspects from the talk (level, enemy, ability, puzzle). An AI asks a few follow-up questions and draws it as a 4-panel storyboard. Teams submit their best version, rate 4 other teams' designs, and the top 3 are revealed on a podium.

This guide covers the whole flow, every screen on the projector, the admin tab and the phones, and a step-by-step playthrough. Click any screenshot to see it full size.

At a glance

The game has five phases. Nothing changes phase on its own: you press the button in the admin tab. The timer is only a display, so when it hits zero nothing happens until you press the next phase button.

1Lobby~2 minHand out code cards. Teams join on one phone each and name themselves.
2Design15 minFill in the design, answer AI questions, get storyboards, redraw, submit.
3Voting4 minEach team rates 4 other designs 1-5 on level, enemy, ability, puzzle.
4Reveal1-2 minYou click 3rd, 2nd, winner. Podium, confetti, trophy on the projector.
5Closed-Gallery of every storyboard with the reflection question: what did the AI get wrong?

The rules teams play by

RuleWhat it means in practice
Teams of 4, one code card eachOne person types on one phone or laptop. Teammates can join with the same code to watch, but each device keeps its own unsaved draft, so only the typist should fill in the form.
500 credits per teamTeams never see dollars. 1 credit = $0.01 of real AI cost, so a storyboard uses about 4-6 credits and a team can redraw dozens of times. Refused attempts use credits too. Shown as "used / 500 credits" and "N left" under the team name. The bar only moves when credits are actually used, not while a drawing is running.
One round of follow-up questionsBefore the first storyboard the AI asks 0-3 short questions (each can be skipped). Redraws go straight to the picture unless the team ticks "Ask me questions again".
One storyboard at a timeA team can't start a second drawing while one is being made.
Submit and resubmitTeams pick one storyboard and submit it. They can switch to a different one until you start voting. Whatever is submitted at that moment gets rated. A team that never submits isn't rated.
VotingEach team gets 4 designs to rate (never its own), anonymously. Text first, art second: "rate the design, not the art". With 7 teams, each design gets exactly 4 ratings.
RankingAverage of the four 1-5 scores across all ratings a design received. Top 3 go on the podium.

What a real storyboard looks like

Every storyboard is one picture with 4 panels: 1 entering the level, 2 the enemy encounter, 3 finding the ability, 4 solving the puzzle at the exit. This one was made on the live site on 11/5 from a quick test design (a flooded research station, an eyeless diver creature that hunts by sound, a sonar pulse, a three-valve puzzle). It took about 15 seconds and cost $0.04.

Phase 2

Design: describe the level, get storyboards, submit

15 minutes by default. This is where the game happens.

3

YouControls → check the Design length (15:00) → Start designing.
PhoneSwitches to the design form, with the countdown and the credits bar in the header.
Projector"Design your level", the big countdown, "Use all four: Level Enemy Ability Puzzle", a tile per team, and "N of M submitted".
4

The team fills in the design form

The form takes the whole screen. After the first generate, the storyboards appear: below the form on laptops, and in a new Storyboard tab on phones. A big "Show, don't tell!" stamp sits at the top of the writing card (and on the projector). The form has two parts:

PartFieldHow it works
The vibe
(dark terminal card)		GenreChips: Horror, Puzzle, Action-adventure, RPG, Platformer, Sci-fi, Fantasy, or "+ Other" (type your own).
Feeling *Free text, with tappable examples: hunted, uneasy, powerful, lonely, curious, frantic.
Art styleFree text, with examples: pixel art, 1930s rubber-hose cartoon, gritty 1970s sci-fi, watercolor storybook, claymation, comic book ink.
The four aspects
(parchment card)		Level *The path from the start to the exit. "How does it create your feeling?"
Enemy *Its look and how it hunts.
Ability *Where it's found and what it lets you do.
Puzzle *What it looks like and what solving it opens.

* required. Any field can also take an optional reference image (up to 4 in total): a screenshot, a photo of a sketch, an art-style example. Big photos are shrunk only if they're over 2048 px, so detail isn't lost. The draft saves itself on that device.

5

Generate → the AI asks follow-up questions

The sticky button at the bottom reads Next: questions, then draw and shows the most this attempt can use ("up to N credits"; the real cost is usually well under that). The AI reads the design (and any reference images) and asks 0-3 short questions about whatever is unclear. Each has an answer field and Skip. Then Draw it. "Never mind, back to editing" cancels.

6

The storyboard arrives → submit it

The storyboard appears as "Version 1" with numbered panels. Tap it (or Zoom) for full screen. Right under the picture is Submit this storyboard. Below that:

  • the four panel captions;
  • "What we changed", if the AI had to soften anything (for example graphic gore made implied, or a famous character turned into an original one);
  • "What the artist was told", a collapsible section with the exact image prompt;
  • cost and time;
  • a History strip of every version.

After submitting, a SUBMITTED stamp appears and the projector tile flips to submitted.

7

Iterate: redraw, compare versions, resubmit

Teams can edit any field and press Redraw storyboard as often as their credits allow. Redraws skip the questions unless "Ask me questions again" is ticked. Tapping a version in History reopens it, and "Edit from this version" loads its text back into the form. To change the submission, open another version and press Submit this one instead.

If the team has storyboards but hasn't submitted, a "Not submitted yet" banner with a Submit button stays in the sticky bar, and it pulses amber in the last 60 seconds.

8

Meanwhile, on your screens

Admin · Teams tabA row per team with its status (drafting, asking, generating, submitted), online or last seen, credits used, number of storyboards, a submitted thumbnail, and View / Reset credits.
Admin · Feed tabA live list: joined, renamed, asked, drew, refused, submitted, voted. Filter chips (All, Generations, Problems, Joins, Submits/votes, Admin).
ProjectorTiles show "SUBMITTED" and the storyboard count, but no pictures (so teams can't copy each other). The last 10 seconds show a giant pulsing countdown, then "Time is up. Submit what you have!".
Timer controls (the "Running now" panel): Pause / Resume freezes the countdown on every screen with a "Paused" label; End now jumps to 0:00 (with a confirm) without changing the phase; -1:00 / +1:00 / +2:00 / +5:00; "Change to" sets an exact time. Teams can keep working after 0:00 until you press Start voting.

CategoryThe question on screen

Test it solo, step by step

You play the presenter and 2-3 teams yourself. Each browser window keeps its own login, so a phone, an incognito window and a second browser (Safari) are three separate teams. Three teams is the minimum for a full podium. Each storyboard costs about $0.05, so a full run is under $1.

  1. Admin. In your main Chrome, open https://play.mottonote.app/admin and paste the admin code. Click Open projector and put the two tabs side by side.
  2. Teams join. On your phone (scan the QR on any card), in an incognito window and in Safari, join with three different cards. Name them. Check that the projector strip and the admin Teams tab list them by number and name only.
  3. Start designing. Set the timer to something short like 5:00, then press Start designing. All three teams switch to the form.
  4. Play one team properly on the phone.
    • Fill in the form; try a reference image.
    • Generate, answer one question, skip one, then Draw it.
    • Zoom, read "What the artist was told", then Submit.
    • Change something, Redraw, then Submit this one instead.
  5. Play the other two quickly. Generate and submit each. Optionally leave one unsubmitted for a bit to see the amber "Not submitted yet" banner.
  6. Watch the admin tab. Feed fills up, the Teams tab shows credits used, View opens a team's history. Try +1:00 and watch the projector and phones follow, then Reset credits on one team.
  7. Start voting. On each team, rate the 2 designs it's given and press All done. The projector shows "3 of 3 teams done".
  8. Go to the podium. Press Reveal 3rd place, 2nd place, the winner. Try Undo one step once and reveal again. Check the phones: "look up", then each team's place.
  9. End and show the gallery. The projector shows the reflection gallery.
  10. Clean up. Controls → Reset event → type RESET. Teams tab empty, projector back to an empty lobby. Ready for class.
Worth noticing while you test: anything confusing or hard to tap on the phone; anything too small to read when the projector tab is fullscreen; whether an iPhone photo uploads as a reference (HEIC photos convert in Safari; other browsers ask for a JPG or screenshot).

Reset, costs and the fallback

Reset event

Wipes everything

All teams, storyboards, uploads, votes and spend are deleted, and every phone is signed out. Your admin session stays. The codes on the cards keep working, so you can reuse the same cards.

Costs

Small

About $0.04-0.06 (4-6 credits) per storyboard. A team's 500 credits cover roughly 80-100 draws. Building and testing the site used about $3 of the $50 monthly OpenAI cap. A full class should cost $5-15.

Fallback

Paper cards

If the site or Wi-Fi fails: use the small paper attendance cards. Each team writes or draws its level, a few present, and the class votes by show of hands.
